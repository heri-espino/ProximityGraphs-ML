## Recovering cyclic tilings through β-skeletons

Héctor Saib Maravillo Gómeza, Gilberto Calvillo Vivesa, Erick Treviño Aguilara,*

aInstitute of Mathematics, National Autonomous University of Mexico, Cuernavaca, Mexico

##### Abstract

There is a class of proximity graphs parameterized by a real number β that are called β-skeletons and which are geometrical graphs whose vertex set V is a discrete set of points in the plane and two points p and q are adjacent if certain region luneβ(p, q) defined by them does not contain any other vertex. Special cases of β-skeletons are the Gabriel Graph (β = 1) and the Relative Neighborhood Graph (β = 2). Cyclic tilings are tilings of the plane whose tiles are convex cyclic polygons that contain the center of the circle in which they are inscribed. Up to our knowledge, this class of tilings has not been studied before. They generalize regular, Archimedean, and k-uniform tilings. We give sufficient conditions for the graph of a cyclic tiling to be a β-skeleton of its vertices. The result is particularly nice for the case of the Gabriel graph. The study of the relationship between tilings arises while studying street networks. This relationship is illustrated briefly.

## 1. Introduction

Understanding urban street networks is relevant because it allows for comparing the mobility capacities of cities, 1]. Street networks are repre-

sented by graphs whose vertices are the intersections and endpoints of the streets, and the edges correspond to street segments; see [2]. If tunnels and overpasses are ignored, by construction, it is a planar graph.

Many studies characterize street networks through measurements and distributions of topological and geometric variables of street networks (see [3, 4] for a general survey). An alternative approach, less explored, is to approximate street networks using proximity graphs. In these graphs, given a set of points in the plane, two points are joined by an edge if a region defined by them does not contain any other point in the set. Some examples are the Gabriel graph (GG), the β-skeleton, and the relative neighborhood graph (RNG), defined in Section 2. Empirical studies showed that the β-skeleton closely approximates the street networks of some cities in the United States and Japan; see [5, 6]. Besides, the β-skeletons reproduce statistical properties of the street networks of 269 cities in the world; see [7]. For

A tiling or tessellation of the plane is a countable family of closed sets with non-empty interior T = {T1, T2, ..}, called tiles, that cover the plane, and their interiors are pairwise disjoint. If the tiles of T are polygons, the vertices and edges of T are the vertices and edges of its tiles. We denote edges of T with Eπ and its vertices with V. A tiling of this sort is edge-to-edge if the intersection between two tiles T and Tj is one of the following options: 1) the empty set, 2) a vertex of Ti and Tj, or 3) an edge of T and Tj; see [15].

Definition 2. Let T = {T1, T2, ...} be an edge-to-edge tiling, with a discrete set of vertices. We define T to be cyclic if each tile T is a convex polygon inscribed in a circle C = C(c, ri) and the center c is in the interior of Ti.

Regular, Archimedean, and k-uniform tilings are special cases of cyclic ones; see definitions and examples in [15]. We show three examples in Figure 3.

## 4. Conclusion

It has been found empirically that β-skeletons approximate well the street networks [5, 6, 7]. One question that arises from such empirical work is why it works so well. The interest in studying tilings starts to address such concern. So, proving that some tessellations are recovered by β-skeletons implies from a theoretical point of view that as far as street networks maintain certain regularity they will be nicely approximated by β-skeletons. Of course, street networks are not tilings and so we think that a better theoretical model is tessellations obtained from cyclic tilings by perturbing its vertex set. This is a more difficult task and it is work in progress.
